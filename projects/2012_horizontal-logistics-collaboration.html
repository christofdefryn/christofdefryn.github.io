Horizontal logistics collaboration

Optimisation models and gain sharing mechanisms for partners cooperating in logistics networks

Horizontal Cooperation Vehicle Routing Gain Sharing

Overview

This PhD project studies how independent logistics partners can cooperate horizontally — sharing vehicles, routes, and delivery zones — to reduce costs and environmental impact while remaining individually satisfied with the outcome. We developed multi-objective and selective vehicle routing formulations that integrate each partner's individual objectives directly into the optimisation model, rather than treating the coalition as a single decision maker.

A second strand of the work focuses on gain sharing: once a coalition's joint cost savings are computed, how should they be distributed among partners in a way that is fair and keeps the coalition stable? This was studied both theoretically and through a case study in the fresh fruit and vegetables sector. Methodological contributions include a two-level variable neighbourhood search for the clustered vehicle routing problem and multi-objective models for the collaborative travelling salesman problem, disseminated across international conferences and journals throughout the project.
